**What the exemplars share**
- Mostly flat, full-bleed presentation. The mark sits on a solid field (yellow, red, black, maroon) with no device mockups, desks, or props.
- Two-color thinking. Black on acid green or yellow, cream on brick red, bright red on deep maroon, blue on mint green. One accent, high contrast, no gradients.
- Type is the hero. Custom or heavily drawn letterforms: swashed display caps, a slanted hand-cut script, soft bubbly caps, tight-fitting ligatures where letters share strokes, and a chunky slab with a square period.
- One clever idea per mark. Looping strokes forming a pinwheel, the two E's in KEEN fused as C shapes, a symbol built from mirrored blobs.
- Generous empty space around a centered mark. Nothing competes.
- When imagery appears, it is treated, not stock-raw. Botanical illustration broken into a pixel mosaic, a dim cinematic photo under ornate type, sound-wave shapes chewing into poster type.
- Texture is subtle, like the slight print grain on the maroon piece.

**By kind of work: what the best look like**
- **Logos:** Mark plus wordmark on a flat field. Drawn letterforms with a visible twist (shared strokes, rounded terminals, a hand-cut feel). A badge works only if the inner symbol is original.
- **Typographic systems:** Dense grids of mixed scripts and weights, as with the huge condensed Latin, Japanese katakana, a serif tagline, and mono labels. Repetition builds a pattern while staying legible and on brand.
- **Identities:** The wordmark is clean and simple, while the image layer carries a distinct treatment (mosaic, grade, overlay). It reads like a frame from a brand film.
- **Posters:** Bold grotesque type colliding with an abstract graphic form, limited to red and black, with deliberate distortion.

**What to mark bad even when it matches the words**
- Logo sales sheets: grids of many unrelated logos, "logo pack" or "template" labels, watermarks, price tags.
- Mockup packs: phones, stationery stacks, tote bags, or shop signs rendered in 3D scenes where the design is an afterthought.
- Stock vector marks: generic swooshes, leaves, globes, and lightbulbs, or initials in a circle using a default font.
- Clip-art or AI-glossy illustrations with gradients, bevels, drop shadows, or many colors.
- Brand guideline pages full of filler text and swatch tables with no strong mark.

**Grading**
- **Good, not great:** Correct format, flat background and simple palette, but the type is an untouched stock font with no idea in the letterforms.
